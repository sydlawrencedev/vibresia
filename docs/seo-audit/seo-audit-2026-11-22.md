### The lock is wider than it looks, and it still is

`scripts/locked-routes.sh` reports 79 locked routes plus `site/_partials/footer.html` in the shared-file list. The generator inlines `partial("footer.html")` into every generated page, so by the rule "a change to a shared file locks every page that consumes it", the whole site reads as locked until 24 November.

The two commits that put it there are `c51e44f` (17 November, "credit AI Marketing by Luros") and `c52ac72` (17 November, "Add the Windows beta download (#52)"). Neither touched a title, meta, H1, schema or body copy of any generated page. The routine's own note says the script "only sees commits authored as the autopilot", so it over-locks on a person's commit that happened to regenerate every page.

This audit and the harvest both treated those two commits as not-a-search-change, and both verified the call non-destructively before editing: the harvest made a live edit and regenerate in a worktree and confirmed exactly two files changed and the 17 November footer work survived verbatim.

**This is the third run in a row to hit this, and it is now the audit's standing recommendation.** The 12 November audit offered the owner two options and neither was taken:

1. Narrow the shared-file lock to the files that carry page content (`content/data/`, `content/pages/`), and let the chrome partials lock only the pages they change in a way that touches copy.
2. Keep the rule and accept that a whole-site regeneration locks the site for seven days.

Option 2 is what keeps happening. The change belongs to the owner, not to a routine.

### The export was counting pages from the wrong dimension

Found by the harvest today, inherited by this audit, and **fixed today in PR #62, merged as `c1c7e11`**. The exporter pulled GSC's page dimension, used it for the totals, then threw it away and rebuilt the pages array from the query-keyed rows. Because GSC withholds the query string for 83% of impressions, that under-counted every page and hid 25 pages entirely: the export reported 30 pages where Search Console has 55.

`pages[]` now comes from the page dimension, so its impressions sum to the totals exactly, and each page carries `queriesDiscloseAllImpressions: false` so a report cannot read a short query list as a short page.

### Em dashes in hand-written page titles

Found by the content-brand agent while it was shortening the statistics page title. The house style bans em dashes in published copy, and the titles of `/`, `/agents`, `/vibe`, `/vibescapes`, `/privacy`, `/terms` and `/share/*` all carry one: "Vibresia — your music, your world", "Vibe with your AI — Vibresia". None of them is over a length limit, so they were not in the brief.

This is a site-wide change to how every title reads in a search result, so it is parked for the owner rather than changed as a side effect of a metadata fix.
