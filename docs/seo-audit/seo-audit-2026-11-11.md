## 4. Content briefs

### 4.1 Shipped

#### Brief: llms.txt was missing four pages the site already had

**The finding.** `site/llms.txt` is the file an answer engine reads to work out what the site is and which pages matter. A page that is in a sitemap but missing from llms.txt is a page an assistant has no reason to open, even though Google can rank it.

The file listed 48 of the 52 sitemap landing pages. Three pages that answer a real question were absent:

| Missing path | The question it answers | Why it was missing |
|---|---|---|
| /blog/plex-visualizer-mac | Does Plex have a visualizer on Mac? | Published in commit 2225f71, "Question pages for Plex and Twitch, and llms.txt that keeps itself". The file did not get them. |
| /blog/twitch-visualizer-mac | Does Twitch have a visualizer? | Same commit, same omission. |
| /blog/parachord-visualizer-mac | Does Parachord have a visualizer? | Published in commit 90530e0, "Parachord is on the list of music apps, with a page of its own". |
| /vibescapes | The gallery of vibescapes | Was `noindex` until 6 November 2026 and was never added when it was indexed. |

This was an oversight, not a curation decision. The file already carried the Apple Music, Spotify, YouTube Music, SoundCloud, TIDAL, Mixcloud, Amazon Music, Deezer and VLC pages, all of them of the same shape as the three missing ones.
**Why it was safe to ship despite the lock.** The 7 day lock covers pages. The three pages above are locked: their `site/` HTML was regenerated on 2026-11-06 and their routes appear in `scripts/locked-routes.sh`. But the change ships is to `site/llms.txt`, which is not a page, does not appear anywhere in the lock output, and was last changed on 2026-11-03, outside the window. The three in-window commits that touch the generator change the comparison page HTML and the gallery sitemap, not the llms.txt content: `llmsReferees` is byte for byte the same before and after.

This was verified rather than assumed. Editing the curated list and running the generator changes exactly one file:

```
$ node scripts/generate-seo-pages.mjs
$ git status --porcelain
 M site/llms.txt
```

No page HTML is regenerated, so no locked page is touched.

**The copy.** From the content-brand agent, via the written-handoff convention in `agents/content-brand/TOOLS.md`. It also corrected the brief: Parachord is a multi-source player with no visualiser of its own, not a video maker, and the shipped line says so.

```
- [The gallery of vibescapes](https://vibresia.com/vibescapes): the ones that come with Vibresia and the ones people have written and shared, newest first, each with its maker's name
- [Does Plex have a visualizer on Mac?](https://vibresia.com/blog/plex-visualizer-mac): partly, Plex has visualisers in Plexamp and the mobile apps as a Plex Pass feature, and what works in the desktop app or browser
- [Does Twitch have a visualizer?](https://vibresia.com/blog/twitch-visualizer-mac): no, Twitch has no visualiser of its own: its graphics are alerts for stream events, so the visuals come from the broadcaster, and how to capture them on a Mac
- [Does Parachord have a visualizer?](https://vibresia.com/blog/parachord-visualizer-mac): no, it is a multi-source player with no visualiser of its own, and Vibresia draws whatever the Mac is playing
```

Every claim in those four lines was checked against the page source and the live page before shipping. The link text quotes the page's own H1, which is written against the "visualizer" query, and the note after the colon uses the house UK spelling "visualiser". That split is the content-brand agent's decision and it matches the existing lines in the file.

**The guard.** `scripts/check-links.mjs` now reports a sitemap page that llms.txt does not mention. That script already runs in CI and in the release gate, so the next omission fails the build instead of passing unnoticed. `scripts/check-links.test.mjs` is new and covers the rule.

**Shipped as:** merge commit `30ca0b612f13169de4f2f2295ae24cf51ef082e4`, PR #42. Landing check reports `LANDED`.

### 4.2 Parked for next rotation

Parked means it is a real finding that could not ship in this run. Each one says which lock it is waiting on and when that lock expires.

#### Two SERP titles are over the 60 character limit

| Page | Current title | Length | Target query | GSC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| /comparison/vibresia-vs-synesthesia | `Synesthesia Alternative for Mac (2026): Vibresia vs Synesthesia` | 63 | synesthesia free alternative | 2 impr, 0 clicks, pos 63.5 |
| /comparison/vibresia-vs-shadertoy | `Shadertoy Alternative for Music (2026): Vibresia vs Shadertoy` | 61 | shadertoy alternative | 0 impr |

The project's stated limit is 60 characters for a title and 158 for a description. The code is slightly looser than the policy: `generate-seo-pages.mjs` throws over 160 for a description, and the test allows a title of 65. Nothing in the repo enforces 60.

**Copy is already settled.** The content-brand agent has written and verified replacements, 55 characters each, keeping the "(2026)" marker and matching the sibling comparison pages:

```
Synesthesia Alternative (2026): Vibresia vs Synesthesia
Vibresia vs Shadertoy (2026): An Alternative for Music?
```

**Why parked.** Both pages are locked, twice over. They are in the comparison cluster, which the harvest routine lists as convention-locked regardless of the git log, and both appear in `scripts/locked-routes.sh`. Their `site/` HTML changed on 2026-11-05 and 2026-11-06 through shared-file changes. The harvest routine is explicit: "If the report says the page is locked, do not edit it."

The brief and the response are filed at `agents/content-brand/work/2026-11-11-seo-title-rewrites.md` and `2026-11-11-seo-title-rewrites-RESPONSE.md`, so the next run is a two line edit.

**Worth being honest about the size of this.** The Synesthesia page has 2 impressions at position 63.5 and the Shadertoy page has none. A title fix will not move either page much on its own. It is worth doing because the titles are over the limit and the fix is nearly free, not because the data says it will help.

#### The lock is wider than it looks, and it may be too wide

Every one of the 54 sitemapped pages, and every page with GSC impressions, is locked this run. `scripts/locked-routes.sh` reports 77 locked routes. The reason is that three commits in the window regenerated the whole site:

| Commit | Date | Pages regenerated |
|---|---|---|
| 9560fdb | 2026-11-05 | 52 |
| c2735d8 | 2026-11-06 | 54 |
| b9dd33c | 2026-11-06 | 51 |

For the two title pages, the actual diff in the window is small: a button colour for contrast, a footer legal-links row, and the author's description inside the schema. **None of it touches the page's own title, H1, description or body copy.**

The lock script's own comment says the rule is right: "A change to a shared file locks every page that consumes it." The rule protects the harvest's work. But a page whose only change was a CSS colour is not a page whose SEO content is in flight, and locking it blocks exactly the title and meta work the SEO routine exists to do.

**This is a question for the harvest routine, not a change to make here.** Two options, both for the owner:

1. Narrow the shared-file lock to the shared files that carry page content (`content/data/`, `content/pages/`), and let the generator and the chrome partials lock only the pages they change in a way that touches copy.
2. Keep the rule as it is, and accept that a whole-site regeneration locks the site for seven days.

The second is what happened this week, and the routine's own instruction to "pick the next unlocked opportunity" had no next opportunity.

#### The comparison cluster is convention-locked, and the blog cluster is not

The harvest routine says the comparison cluster and the provider cluster are convention-locked, and that `/blog/*`, `/guides/*` and `/locations/*` deliberately are not. The blog pages are locked only by the git log, and that log says they were regenerated on 2026-11-06, so they unlock on 2026-11-13.

This matters for the next rotation, because the pages with impressions are mostly blog pages. `/blog/apple-music-visualizer` has 14 of the site's 318 impressions and the largest spread of positions, and it is a blog page. It is the first thing to look at on 2026-11-13.

#### `/blog/apple-music-visualizer` needs a content-depth answer, not a title answer

This is the one Group B page. It has the site's largest impression count (14) and the widest position spread, from 5.0 to 99.0. Google is testing it against several queries and is not satisfied.

The page is 1,319 words with a direct answer at the top, an FAQ block, and full schema. It is not thin. The spread suggests the page is being considered for "apple music visualizer" (position 54.29, 7 impressions, the largest single query on the site) and not placed.

**Do not write a title brief for this yet.** Two impressions at position 99 for "music visualizer iphone" and one at 46 for "mac music visualizer" are two different searches with two different intents, and the page cannot serve both. This needs a decision about which query the page is for, which is a content-strategy call rather than an SEO audit call. Parked until the lock expires on 2026-11-13 and more data has accumulated.
