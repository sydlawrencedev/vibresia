## 1. Headline

**Three findings, in the order they should be acted on.**

**First, the review theme that matters crossed the bar, and it crossed it hard.** Eleven of
the eighteen 1 and 2-star competitor reviews in this 30-day window are the same complaint:
the free tier is a demo, not the product. **Five of the eleven are Vibely**, the Mac music
visualiser on the App Store, and they land in one week. Vibely put a paywall in front of
the export, and the reviewers said so plainly: "Developer got greedy and put it behind a
paywall. Was way better when it was free" (16 November, 1 star). That is a page brief, in
section 5.1.

**Second, two new Mac competitors launched inside the window, and both are in our orbit.**
**Visualinst** shipped on 31 October 2026 and **Polar Static** on 3 November 2026, both on
the Mac App Store, both free to download, both audio-reactive. Two more landed in the ten
days after that: DJ Duo on 16 November and Beethoven's Ninth on 17 November. The Mac App
Store now carries at least twenty dedicated music visualisers. Neither new launch has 20
impressions of brand demand, so neither gets a comparison page; both go on the hub page.
Brief in section 5.2.

**Third, two competitor brands crossed the search bar for the first time.** Resolume is on
27 impressions and TouchDesigner on 23, against 8 each on 12 November. Every one of those
impressions is a head-to-head query we already have a comparison page for, so **no "5
alternatives" post is written for either.** The detail and the reasoning are in section 6.

**Two things the previous two scans got wrong, and this run corrects.** The App Store review
feed works today. The 11 November and 12 November scans both recorded it as returning zero
entries; it returned 50 dated reviews per app this morning, and Google Play turned out to
serve twenty dated reviews per listing in the page data. The counts in section 3 are
therefore the complete most-recent sets for the apps that have them, not the three
highlighted reviews the earlier runs could see. And the reviews the 12 November scan called
1-star, on LIQUIDWAVE and on Prism, are **2 stars**. Both are confirmed twice, from the App
Store page data and from the review feed. The theme counts do not change, but the record
does.

## 2. New competitors

The bar in `docs/competitors.md`: a new product joins the set when it launches and is usable
on macOS, and does one of live music-reactive rendering on Mac, shader-based visuals an AI
can write, or a free Mac visualiser that plays out of the box.

### 2.1 Met the bar, launched inside this window

#### Visualinst (Hahn Yim), App Store id 6821277111

- **What it is.** A music visualiser that analyses a song you load from Files, on the
  device, and draws it: "every kick, snare, bass note and melody". The app works out tempo,
  beats, five frequency bands and the sections of the arrangement, and in Pro every note.
  Its free tier has two looks, a classic visualiser where "a trunk that grows with the beat,
  branches for every hit" and a spiral map of the whole song.
- **Launch.** **31 October 2026**, version 1.0, on the US App Store, with an update on
  5 November. That is inside this window.
- **Platform and price.** A Mac app that requires **macOS 27.0 or later**. Free to download.
  The listing states that "Visualinst Pro is an auto-renewing subscription, monthly or
  yearly", and the App Store page carries US$4.99, US$9.99 and US$99.00 in-app purchase
  prices.
- **Why it matters.** It is the closest thing to Vibresia's shape that has appeared in this
  window, and the closest thing to the paywall problem the review theme describes. Its
  "LIVE" mode is the part that hears "music playing in other apps on your device, or the
  room through the microphone", and **LIVE is a Pro feature**. The free tier analyses a file
  you pick. It also requires an account: "Sign in with your email (a one-time link, no
  password) and Pro works on the website too."
- **Sources.** [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/visualinst-music-visualizer/id6821277111),
  [terms](https://visualinst.com/terms), [privacy](https://visualinst.com/privacy).
- **New competitor bar check.** Launched 31 October 2026 in Global, usable on macOS, and it
  is a free Mac visualiser that plays out of the box. **Met. Propose adding to
  `docs/competitors.md`.**
- **Search demand check.** The live Search Console export for the 28 days to 20 November
  2026 contains **no query containing "visualinst"**. **Not met. No "5 alternatives to
  Visualinst" post.**

#### Polar Static (Ihar Klionski), App Store id 6826664120

- **What it is.** "Living art for your screens: smoke, liquid, particles and light that
  move on their own, hear your music and answer your touch." Three modes: Ambient, which
  needs no music at all; Reactive, which listens through the microphone or to music playing
  on the same iPhone; and Touch.
- **Launch.** **3 November 2026**, version 1.0.0, on the US App Store. Inside this window.
- **Platform and price.** A Mac app requiring **macOS 26.0 or later**. Free to download,
  with in-app purchases at US$0.99, US$2.99, US$5.99, US$7.99 and US$9.99.
- **Why it matters, and where it does not.** It is a Mac-compatible free audio-reactive
  app, so it meets the bar. But it is not a listening visualiser in our sense. Its Reactive
  mode is microphone-based, it is framed as ambient art rather than as a visualiser for the
  music you are playing, and its own description points at "a spare iPad" and a TV. The
  honest comparison is with a screensaver, not with Vibresia.
- **Sources.** [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/polar-static-music-visualizer/id6826664120).
- **New competitor bar check.** Launched 3 November 2026 in Global, usable on macOS, free
  Mac visualiser that plays out of the box. **Met, on a technicality. Propose adding to
  `docs/competitors.md` and to the hub page, not a comparison page.**
- **Search demand check.** **No query containing "polar static" or "polarstatic" in the
  export.** **Not met.**

### 2.3 Corrections to records already in the set

Three numbers in circulation are wrong. Two of them are already recorded; one is new. None
of them is a page brief. They are guardrails, and they belong in any copy that picks them
up.

- **MilkDrop 3 exists, and it is maintained.** Our record in `content/data/competitors.json`
  says "the last released binary is MilkDrop 2.0d from January 2008, and the only later
  version is the 2.25c source code of 15 May 2013, so no new build has been issued in over
  thirteen years". That is true of Ryan Geiss's MilkDrop and false of the product now called
  MilkDrop 3. Its own site says: "MilkDrop 3 is an incredible audio visualizer packed with
  over 500 stunning visuals", "It works with any audio source, including Spotify, YouTube,
  SoundCloud, Winamp, and more", "Fully compatible with Windows 7, 8, 10, and 11", and
  "You are encouraged to use and share the free version for any non-remunerated purposes.
  Please get the PRO version for anything remunerated". Source:
  [milkdrop3.com](https://milkdrop3.com/).
  **What this changes.** `docs/competitors.md` describes MilkDrop as "Free for any use, with
  no output cap and no licence tiers". That is no longer true of the MilkDrop name in the
  wild, and the page copy on `/blog/milkdrop-alternatives-mac-2026` and
  `/blog/does-milkdrop-work-on-mac` needs to distinguish Geiss's MilkDrop from MilkDrop 3.
  MilkDrop 3 is Windows only, so it is not a new Mac competitor, but the pages that claim
  "nothing has been maintained since 2008" are now contestable.
- **Synesthesia's price.** Still US$199 / US$399 against `synesthesia.live/pricing`. Still
  wrong in Arkestra's listicle, which prices it "US$129 to US$259".
- **Arkestra's own price.** Still wrong in Arkestra's own listicle, which says "free tier +
  $199 Pro". Arkestra's site says: "Rent-to-Own at $19/month for 10 months: reach 10
  payments and it is yours forever" and "Pro is $149 one-time, perpetual license, all 3.x
  updates included, use on 2 computers". **Correct figure: US$149 one-time.**
- **New this run: RENDERWAVE understates Phosphor's price.** The RENDERWAVE guide
  "The Best Mac Music Visualizers in 2026" (30 August 2026) lists Phosphor at "$4.99
  one-time". Phosphor's own support page says "Get Phosphor" for "$6.99" and its footer
  reads "$6.99 · ONE-TIME · MAC APP STORE". Our record says 6.99. **Phosphor is $6.99.** If any of our copy picks up
  $4.99 from a third-party page, correct it against `getphosphor.com/support`.

they agree on every review they share.

HTML carries an `AF_initDataCallback` block keyed `ds:10` holding twenty reviews with epoch
timestamps and star ratings. The earlier scans said Play served "three highlighted reviews
each", which was true of the visible page and false of the data behind it. Twenty listings
were read this run.

history of those apps, not a truncation: LIQUIDWAVE (id 6807689549) and Prism (id 6798428433)
each have a single review on the App Store.
