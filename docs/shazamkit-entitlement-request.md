# The ShazamKit entitlement

Vibresia would like to name the song that is playing by listening to it.
Apple decides that on the app's identifier, and for an app distributed
outside the App Store the answer is an entitlement that has to be granted —
there is no switch in the developer portal to turn it on.

Until it is granted, the app asks Apple's catalogue once, is told
`com.apple.ShazamCore error 102` — "not allowed" — and switches the feature
off, saying why. Nothing is sent and nothing breaks. The request below is
what gets that entitlement granted.

### What the app does

Vibresia draws the screen that goes with the music playing on the Mac. It
works out what is playing so that the picture suits the track — it knows the
genre, and the genre decides the stage the band plays on.

It finds the song two ways, side by side:

1. **By name.** Apple Music and Spotify post a notification when the track
   changes, and the app looks the title and artist up in Apple's public
   catalogue, which knows the genre.
2. **By listening.** ShazamKit matches a signature of the sound against
   Shazam's catalogue. This is the one being requested, and it covers the
   cases the first one cannot: a browser playing a track, a film, a game, a
   music app whose song is not in the public catalogue. Everything that makes
   a sound, rather than only the players that announce themselves.

The second one is off by default. It is a setting in the app, and it is off
until somebody turns it on.

### What is sent, and what is not

Only a ShazamKit **signature** — a one-way fingerprint of about four seconds
of audio, generated by Apple's own framework, which cannot be turned back
into sound. The audio itself never leaves the Mac: it is analysed locally
and drawn locally.

The signature goes to Apple's Shazam service, which does the matching and
returns the title, the artist and the genres. Those are used by the app and
are not stored by us. We do not proxy, log, or keep any copy of it.

This is the same data Apple's own Shazam feature sends, and it is the same
fingerprint that Apple's MusicKit and ShazamKit frameworks produce for any
app using them.

### What happens without it

Without the entitlement the app is refused: `SHSession` answers with
`com.apple.ShazamCore error 102`, which means the app is not allowed to ask
rather than that the catalogue did not know the song. The app handles that
gracefully — it stops asking and says so in its settings — so shipping
without the entitlement is safe, and no audio is sent at all.

## Notes for us, not for Apple

- **Where to send it:** <https://developer.apple.com/contact/> — the form
  that mentions entitlements or a special entitlement. If it wants a
  "Developer ID" distribution type, that is the one to pick.
- **What to attach:** the notarization ID of a recent release, so they can
  see the signature is a real one. `xcrun notarytool history --keychain-profile vibresia-notary`
  prints the submissions and their IDs.
- **How long it takes:** Apple's entitlement requests are answered by a
  person, usually within a few days.
- **What we do while waiting:** nothing is blocked. The feature is in the
  app, behind a setting that is off, and it degrades to "not allowed" and
  says so.
- **When it is granted:** rebuild with `SHAZAMKIT=1 ./release.sh` — the
  entitlement has to be declared in the signature by hand for a Developer ID
  build (see `VisualiserMac/build-app.sh`), and a build without it is
  killed by macOS at launch rather than degrading.
- **Check it worked:** `./build/Vibresia.app/Contents/MacOS/Vibresia --shazam-spike`
  with something playing prints the match, or the error. The answer to watch
  for is a title rather than `error 102`.

## Why the entitlement is opt-in in the build

`VisualiserMac/build-app.sh` only adds the entitlement when it is asked for:

```bash
SHAZAMKIT=1 ./release.sh
```

Declaring an entitlement the team has not been granted is not a build that
fails to match — it is a build that will not launch. macOS kills the
process:

```
$ SHAZAMKIT=1 ./build-app.sh debug && ./build/Vibresia.app/Contents/MacOS/Vibresia
Killed (exit 137)
```

The same app without the entitlement runs, and the match comes back
`com.apple.ShazamCore error 102` — "not allowed", not "not found". So the
entitlement stays out of every build until Apple grants it.
