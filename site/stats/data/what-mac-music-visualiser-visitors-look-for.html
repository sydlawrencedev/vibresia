What people look for when they look for a Mac music visualiser

Most of the search traffic that reaches a Mac visualiser site arrives with a comparison in mind. "MilkDrop vs projectM", "is there a Resolume for Mac". That is the shape of the query, and it is the shape the comparison pages are built for.

But it is not the shape people walk away with. Here is what our own traffic did between 19 October and 12 November 2026.

The findings

215 of the 660 people who visited vibresia.com in those 25 days reached a shared vibescape, which is 32.6% of everybody who came to the site and more than twice as many as reached a comparison page.

101 of those 660 visitors reached a comparison page, 15.3% of the total, and that was fewer than the 285 who reached the homepage.

43.2% of visitors, 285 people, reached the homepage first.

What was counted, and what it does not mean

The cohort is 660 distinct people who loaded a page on vibresia.com in the 25 days from 19 October to 12 November 2026, counted up to 14:00 UTC on the twelfth. A person is counted once per kind of page they reached. Nobody is identifiable in the data: the site's analytics gives each browser a random identifier kept in that browser's own storage, and nothing else is recorded about the visit beyond the page.

Three things this is not.

It is not a sample of people looking for a music visualiser. It is people who found Vibresia. They searched for something and chose this site, so they are selected twice over before we count them. The numbers describe this site's audience, not the market for Mac visualisers.

It is not a preference ranking. The site records which pages people reach, not which ones they like. A page reached twice counts the same as a page reached once. Nothing here says anything about which vibescape is the best one.

It is not the whole audience. The site's measurement can be switched off, in the footer or by a Do Not Track setting, and an ad blocker stops it too. People who blocked it are not in the denominator either. Every ratio here is a ratio between the people we can see.

Method

Who is counted: every distinct browser that loaded a page on vibresia.com between 19 October and 12 November 2026, counted up to 14:00 UTC on the twelfth. That is 660 people.

When: the 25 days from 19 October to 12 November 2026 inclusive.

How: the site's own anonymous analytics, held in PostHog. Each page view is counted once per browser, and a browser is counted once per kind of page it reached. Our own test account, our own internal traffic and our uptime probes are excluded, which removes 6 people in total.

Cohort size: 660 visitors. The smallest cell published above is 52.

Known biases: these are people who found Vibresia, not a sample of anybody. The window is 25 days long and it is a launch-period window, so it is not a steady state. The measurement can be blocked, so the denominator is the people we can see. A visitor can appear in two rows of the table. One person is one browser, so somebody who clears their browser storage and comes back counts twice.

There is no public benchmark for this, so there is nothing to compare it against. Published engagement figures for 2026 are platform numbers, measured on TikTok and LinkedIn, and they are not measuring the same thing.
